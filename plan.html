Making the draft approachable

An editorial review of Music Theory Through Sonic Pi at commit 3baa1e6, and a plan for the third draft. The prose problem is not a writing problem. It is a specification problem, and it is fixable at the source.

58 lessons · 5 appendices 62,381 words 41,160 words of lesson prose Review only — no files changed
Verdict 1. Why it sounds cold 2. The root cause 3. Rewrite STYLE.md 4. Before / after 5. Break the template 6. Content gaps 7. Specific defects 8. Work sequence 9. Do not change

§0Verdict

The book is better than it reads. The musical content is largely correct, the score/renderer spine is genuinely good pedagogy, and the honesty about what has and has not been auditioned is a real virtue. What it lacks is a person.

You said you cannot get the other agent to sound approachable. I can show you why, and it is not a matter of asking harder. Two artefacts in this repository actively manufacture the voice you dislike, and any agent that obeys them will reproduce it every time:

  • STYLE.md is a list of prohibitions. It bans twenty words, five constructions, praise, cheer, rhetorical questions, em dashes, and — decisively — the writer's own first person. It supplies almost no positive model of the target voice. An agent given a ban list optimises for not being caught, which produces prose that is inoffensive and inert.
  • tests/test_book_draft.py enforces the sameness. REQUIRED_HEADINGS hard-codes the same five section titles into all 58 lessons and a minimum word count pushes every lesson to the same length. The template is not a habit the agent fell into. It is a passing test.

Change those two files and the prose changes. Leave them and you will keep re-litigating the voice one lesson at a time, forever.

§1Why it sounds cold: six measurements

These are counts over the 41,160 words of lesson prose with all code blocks stripped out. Each one is a specific, addressable habit rather than a matter of taste.

0
verbal contractions in the whole book. All 85 apostrophes are possessives (chord's, note's). Not one don't, you'll, here's, that's.
8
question marks across 58 lessons. The book never once wonders aloud or asks you to predict something before it tells you.
2.4×
per lesson that the word "you" appears. "We" appears zero times in 58 lessons, though STYLE.md explicitly permits it.
~26%
of sentences open with "The" or "A" plus an abstract noun. The grammatical subject is almost always a thing, never a person.
567
hedges (may, can, remains, whether) — about 14 per thousand words. Roughly one hedge every four sentences.
14.8 ± 6.1
words per sentence. 75% of sentences fall between 10 and 25 words; only 11% are under 8. No sentence ever lands hard.

1.1 The register is locked formal

Zero contractions is the single loudest signal. It is what separates "you'll hear the third drift" from "the third may be heard to drift," and readers register it long before they can name it. Combined with 26 nominalisations per thousand words (-tion, -ment, -ness, -ity), which is academic-journal density, the surface never relaxes.

1.2 The hedging is the biggest single problem

This one deserves its own diagnosis, because it comes from a good intention. Nothing has been auditioned in Sonic Pi, so the draft honestly refuses to claim what anything sounds like. But that honesty leaked out of the metadata and into every sentence:

"The ratio-built version is expected to have less internal motion around its third; the equal-tempered version may carry a faint shimmer. Those expectations still require audition on the stated synth and playback system." — Lesson 6

A reader at a keyboard needs to know what to listen for. Told only what might happen, they cannot tell a failed setup from a subtle effect from a wrong claim. The prose sounds evasive precisely where it is trying to be scrupulous. The fix is structural, not verbal: commit in the prose, and quarantine the uncertainty into one visible per-lesson marker. See §3.3.

1.3 Banning a construction did not remove the reflex

STYLE.md bans "not X, but Y". The draft contains 108 instances of rather than and instead of — the identical corrective move wearing a different coat. The lesson generalises: prohibitions redirect a habit, they do not dissolve it. Only a positive model does that.

1.4 There is nobody in the book

STYLE.md permits first person singular only for documented human observation. Since no human has audited anything yet, the rule evaluates to a total ban in practice: 24 instances of "I" in 41,160 words. The preface promises "an informed person at the next desk." No such person exists in the text. The result reads like well-written API documentation for music.

§2The root cause, stated plainly

Your style guide is a slop filter, and it works: the prose has no filler, no cheerleading, no delve, no unlock. But a slop filter is not a voice. It defines the floor and says nothing about the ceiling, so an agent writes to the floor and stops.

The guide is also self-defeating in three specific places:

STYLE.md ruleIntentWhat it actually produced
First person only for documented human observationDon't fabricate listening experienceNo narrator at all. 24 "I"s in 41k words.
"Do not praise, scold, cheer"No hypeNo warmth of any kind. Affect stripped to zero.
Rhetorical questions "only when the pause does work"No filler questions8 question marks in 58 lessons. The reader is never invited to predict.
Em dash: "prefer a full stop, comma, or colon"Reduce a known AI ticLinted to zero. Every aside now becomes its own flat sentence, flattening rhythm.
"Distinguish an observed result from an expected result"Honesty about untested claims14 hedges per 1,000 words. Nothing is ever asserted.
REQUIRED_HEADINGS + 550-word floor in pytestEnsure lessons are substantive58 lessons of identical shape and near-identical length.

Every one of these was a reasonable response to a real failure mode. The problem is that the corrections are all subtractive, and six subtractive rules compounded produce a book with nothing sticking out of it anywhere.

§3Change 1: rewrite STYLE.md from prohibition to demonstration

This is the highest-leverage change in the plan and should happen before a single lesson is touched. Target: cut the ban list roughly in half, and spend the recovered space on worked examples of the voice you want.

3.1 Add a positive voice specification

Open the guide with a passage of the target voice, annotated. Then state the mandates as things to do:

  • Use contractions. Default to them in explanation. Reserve the full form for emphasis.
  • Make the reader or the writer the subject of the sentence at least once per paragraph. Not "the program schedules six attacks" but "you get six evenly spaced attacks."
  • Open at least one paragraph per lesson with a question the reader can answer by pressing Run.
  • One sentence under eight words per section. Rhythm is a requirement, not a nicety. The current 11% needs to roughly double.
  • Name the surprise. Every lesson has one moment where the sound contradicts the naive expectation. Say so, in those words.

3.2 Restore the em dash, with a budget

Banning it outright cost more than it saved. Replace the ban with a quota the linter can enforce: at most one em dash per lesson. That keeps the tic in check while returning the one mark that carries an aside without breaking the sentence.

3.3 Replace pervasive hedging with a single honesty marker

This is the change that will most transform how the book reads. Make it a hard rule:

In the body text, state what the reader will hear, in the present indicative. All uncertainty about verification lives in exactly two places: the status: field in front matter, and a short standard callout at the top of the lesson. Zero hedges in the explanation itself.

Concretely: add a shared Quarto include, _not-yet-auditioned.qmd, that renders one small callout, and delete every in-sentence "may", "is expected to", "remains a listening judgement" from the prose. The book becomes more honest, not less — one clear disclosure beats 567 mumbled ones, and when a lesson is auditioned you remove one line instead of re-editing forty sentences.

3.4 Loosen the first-person rule to permit reasoning, not experience

The rule correctly forbids inventing listening history. It should not forbid the writer's presence. Permit first person for authorial decisions, which are real and documentable: "I've kept the bass out of this example because it hides the third." Forbid it for sensory claims until a human has listened. That single distinction gives the book a narrator without licensing a fabrication.

3.5 Update the linter to match

scripts/lint-prose.sh currently only catches bad words. Add positive checks so the new mandates are enforceable rather than aspirational: per-lesson contraction count above zero, at least one sentence under eight words per section, hedge density below a threshold, em dashes at most one per file, and a warning when more than 30% of sentences in a file start with "The" or "A".

§4Change 2: six worked rewrites to paste into STYLE.md

These are the models the guide is missing. Each deliberately breaks a current rule — contractions, direct address, an em dash, a question, a committed claim — and each is drawn verbatim from the present draft.

4.1 The opening of a lesson

Now — Lesson 6

"A tuning system chooses which frequency relationships remain exact and where small discrepancies will appear. Pure fifths make a tempting foundation: multiply a frequency by 3/2 twelve times and the resulting pitch comes close to seven octaves above the start. It does not arrive exactly."

Proposed

"Stack twelve pure fifths and you land almost exactly seven octaves up. Almost. Multiply by 3/2 twelve times and you overshoot by about a quarter of a semitone — nothing at all on one chord, and a disaster once you want to play in all twelve keys. That gap is the Pythagorean comma, and every tuning system ever built is a decision about where to hide it."

What changed: a two-word sentence for rhythm, second person, a committed number (23.5 cents), and a closing line that says why the reader should care before the terminology arrives.

4.2 The paragraph after the code

Now — Lesson 6

"Both triads implement a major-third and fifth structure, although the audible contrast may be subtler than the numerical formulas suggest. The ratio-built version is expected to have less internal motion around its third; the equal-tempered version may carry a faint shimmer."

Proposed

"Listen to the third, not the whole chord. In the ratio-built triad it sits still. In the equal-tempered one it shimmers, because that third is 14 cents sharp of a pure 5:4 and the two tones keep drifting in and out of phase. If you hear nothing on the first pass, you're not doing it wrong: this difference lives close to the floor on small speakers."

What changed: an instruction first, then two committed claims, then a specific number that makes the claim checkable, then a contraction doing the reassurance the ban list forbade. Note that this rewrite also exposes a real bug in the lesson — see §7.1.

4.3 The explanation

Now — Lesson 41

"Provenance belongs beside the score data. Record edition, page, system, and measure even if those fields never reach Sonic Pi. When a note sounds doubtful, that pointer leads directly back to the evidence."

Proposed

"Write the edition, page, system, and measure into the score, even though Sonic Pi will never read them. Six months from now a note will sound wrong and you won't remember whether you mis-copied it or the editor did. Those four fields are the difference between checking and guessing."

What changed: the abstract noun "provenance" stops being the subject; the reader is. The claim is dramatised with a scenario rather than asserted.

4.4 The transition to the next lesson

Now — Lesson 5

"Pure ratios work beautifully when one root and a few intervals define the local world. The next lesson asks what happens when a keyboard must support the same interval pattern from every one of twelve starting notes."

Proposed

"All of this works while you stay put. Tune a perfect fifth above C and it's exact. But now the piece moves to D, and then to E-flat, and your keyboard has only twelve keys to serve all of them. Something has to give. Lesson 6 is about what gives, and by how much."

4.5 The exercise

Now — Lesson 38

"Generate a Markdown table for positions -2 through 10. Include coordinate, line-or-space classification, letter, octave, Sonic Pi symbol, and MIDI integer, and mark middle C plus the five treble lines."

Proposed

"Build yourself the reference card you'd actually want taped to the monitor: positions -2 to 10, with the letter, the octave, the Sonic Pi symbol, and the MIDI number for each, and middle C and the five treble lines marked. Print it. You'll use it for the whole of Part 8."

STYLE.md already warns against "bureaucratic exercise language" and the draft still produces it, because the guide names the fault without modelling the alternative. Fifty-eight Take it further sections need this treatment.

4.6 The variation instruction

Now — Lesson 1

"Replace :pluck with :prophet. The note symbols and sleeps do not move, but the softened attack makes their exact starting points less prominent and the broader tail fills more of the gap between them."

Proposed

"Swap :pluck for :prophet and run it again. Not one note or sleep has changed, so anything different you hear belongs to the instrument. What you lose is the click of each onset; what you gain is a tail long enough to fill the gap before the next note. Same phrase, different body."

§5Change 3: break the template

The uniformity is measurable and total. It is also the thing that most makes the book feel machine-made, independent of any individual sentence.

PropertyLessons conforming
Exactly the headings The idea / Run this / Change one thing / Why it works / Take it further, in that order58 / 58
Exactly one code block58 / 58
Exactly three numbered variations57 / 58
Length between 724 and 1,011 words58 / 58
Zero images, figures, diagrams, or tables58 / 58

What to do

  1. Relax the pytest contract. In test_book_draft.py, replace the exact REQUIRED_HEADINGS tuple with a check that a lesson contains a runnable example, at least one prompted variation, and an exercise — however those are titled. Keep the 58-lesson and solutions-coverage invariants; they are earning their keep.
  2. Let section titles say what the section is about. "Why it works" × 58 is a filing system. "Why twelve fifths miss the octave" tells a reader scanning the page what they are about to learn. Keep the underlying rhythm; vary the labels.
  3. Allow two or three code blocks where the lesson needs them. At present every "Change one thing" instruction asks for a hand-edit with no shown result. Where a variation matters, show the changed block.
  4. Vary length deliberately. Lessons 2, 10 and 14 could be 400 words. Lessons 31, 43 and 51 want 1,400. Uniform length means every lesson is padded or truncated toward the same number.
  5. Give each Part a one-page opener. Thirteen short bridges would give the book the sense of movement that 58 identical units currently deny it, at maybe 2,000 words total.

§6Content gaps

6.1 The book about sound has no sound high

There are 60 audio="" attributes and zero recordings. This is a known and deliberately flagged state, so it is not a criticism of honesty — but it is the largest single gap between what the book promises and what it delivers. Every listening claim is currently unfalsifiable for the reader. Rendering even one reference recording per Part (13 files) would change the experience more than any prose edit.

6.2 The book about notation has no notation high

There is not one image, figure, diagram, or table in 58 lessons and 5 appendices. Most lessons survive this. Part 8 does not:

  • Lesson 38, "Treble clef as a coordinate system," describes lines and spaces in words and asks the reader to hold a five-line staff in their head.
  • Lesson 41, "Decode four printed bars," contains no printed bars. The exercise sends the reader off to find their own public-domain score. The lesson cannot be completed from the book.

Fix: generate SVG staff examples with LilyPond or Verovio into assets/img/, checked in as build products. Minimum viable set is about eight figures — treble staff with coordinates, grand staff, the four key signatures used, and four printed bars for Lesson 41 sourced from a public-domain edition. Elsewhere, a handful of ASCII or SVG grids would carry rhythm (Part 3), the circle of fifths (Lesson 25), and the piano keyboard map (Lesson 8).

6.3 Cross-references are text, not links medium

47 bare "Lesson N" mentions in the prose against 3 actual markdown links. In an HTML book with a working site-url, every one of those should be clickable. This is mechanical and scriptable.

6.4 The three capstones need a shared piece of music medium

Lessons 55–57 each invent their own material. A book whose spine is "the representation grows because the music asks more of it" should end by returning to something the reader already knows — ideally the E-minor-pentatonic phrase from setup.qmd, now carrying harmony, voices and form. That single decision would make the arc land.

6.5 Ear-training drills are described but never centralised low

The preface commits the reader to 10 minutes a day from their own drills.rb, and Lesson 20 builds a drill harness, but no lesson afterward adds to it. Either make "add this to drills.rb" a recurring one-line coda, or drop the daily-practice promise from the preface.

§7Specific defects worth fixing regardless of voice

Found by reading, not by running — none of this has been executed in Sonic Pi, and it should all be confirmed on audition. Each is a concrete claim you can check.

7.1 Lesson 6 demonstrates tuning with the one synth that cannot show it high

The lesson compares a just triad against an equal-tempered one using synth :sine. A sine wave has a single partial. The audible difference between just and equal-tempered intonation comes almost entirely from beating between coincident upper partials — the root's 5th harmonic against the third's 4th, for instance. Remove the harmonics and you remove the phenomenon. Two sines four semitones apart, heard in separate passes, will sound essentially identical whichever tuning produced them.

The lesson's own closing paragraph has the reasoning backwards: it warns that changing synth may introduce "new partials or attacks" that "conceal the gentle beating." Partials are what create the beating here. Fix: use :saw, :tri or :prophet and sustain the chords longer. Lesson 5, by contrast, is right to use sines — near-unison beating is exactly what sines show best.

7.2 Lesson 16's third variation silently breaks its own seed medium

The lesson correctly warns that "the seed reproduces a comparison only while the order and mapping of random calls remain unchanged." Variation 3 then asks the reader to "set amplitude variation to zero while leaving timing variation intact" and compare with the original. Removing those two rrand calls per pair shifts every subsequent draw in the seeded stream, so the timing jitter changes too and the comparison is not controlled. Fix: draw all random values into named variables up front, so removing one from use does not renumber the rest — which is also a better lesson about seeded randomness.

7.3 Lesson 29's cadences all move in strict parallel motion medium

Every chord is built with chord(root, quality) in root position and close voicing, so IV → I moves all three voices up exactly 7 semitones and V → I moves all three up exactly 5. Parallel octaves and fifths throughout. That is awkward on its own terms, and it is awkward against Lesson 31, which teaches that cadences are four lines moving independently. It also means the tonic chord sits an octave above the IV, V and vi chords, so "arrival" is confounded with a register jump. Fix: hand-voice the four cadences with a common tone held.

Separately, body ends on :IV, so the plagal ending produces IV IV I and the half cadence produces IV IV V. Two of the four conditions have a repeated chord at the seam that the other two don't.

7.4 Lesson 31's "well-led" voicing puts bass and tenor in unison low

In the led progression, the G chord is bass: 55, tenor: 55. Two of the four voices are the same pitch, so the chord sounds in three parts and the tenor line vanishes for a beat — in a lesson whose subject is tracking four independent lines. The movement cost function cannot see this, which is a genuinely good teaching point if you make it deliberate: add a "no unisons" constraint and let the reader discover why the optimiser wanted one.

7.5 The hz_to_midi round trip low

Lessons 5 and 6 compute exact frequencies and then pass them through hz_to_midi into note:. Worth confirming on audition that Sonic Pi preserves the fractional MIDI value rather than rounding it — the entire premise of both lessons is that sub-semitone differences survive to the output.

§8Suggested sequence

Ordered so that nothing gets written twice. The voice work is worthless until the specification changes, and the specification is worthless until the tests stop enforcing the old one.

On step 4, the one to resist skipping. The reason the agent can't sound approachable is that it has been given a definition of the voice and no instance of it. Seven hand-written lessons are worth more than seven more pages of style rules, and they are the only input that reliably transfers. If you write nothing else yourself, write Lesson 1 and Lesson 6.

§9What not to change

A rewrite this broad can easily damage what already works. These are load-bearing.

  • The score/renderer split. Introduced in Lesson 1 and carried through to Lesson 58. It is the strongest idea in the book and the reason a programmer will keep reading.
  • The :note / :start / :duration event schema. Consistent across all 58 lessons — genuinely impressive continuity, and hard to re-establish if broken.
  • Sound before name. The curriculum ordering is well judged: acoustics before naming, notation at Part 8 rather than Part 1, transcription last. Do not let a voice pass disturb the sequence.
  • The draft / tested distinction and the AI-authorship disclosure. The intellectual honesty here is unusual and worth keeping. §3.3 concentrates it; it does not remove it.
  • The banned-vocabulary list. Keep it. It works. Just stop treating it as the whole of the style guide.
  • The musical content. Spot-checks across acoustics, cadences, voice leading, swing and clef translation found the theory sound and the explanations accurate. The problem really is only how it sounds on the page.
